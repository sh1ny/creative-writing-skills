# Beat Writing Guidance

## What Is a Beat?

A **beat** is the atomic unit of prose in the scene-writing workflow. It is:
- One moment of story (an action, reaction, dialogue exchange, or emotional shift)
- Contained in continuous real-time (no time jumps)
- Written from a single POV character's perspective
- 100-500 words typically, though length varies by moment

**Beat ≠ Scene ≠ Chapter:**
- **Chapter** — Contains multiple scenes, spans multiple locations/time periods
- **Scene** — Contains multiple beats, takes place in one location over one time period
- **Beat** — One moment within a scene

Example: A confrontation scene might have beats like:
1. "Aria enters the tavern and scans the room"
2. "She spots Marcus in the corner, approaches carefully"
3. "Marcus recognizes Orin and tensions spike"
4. "The argument explodes"

---

## Beat Writing Principles

### One Moment Per Beat

Each beat dramatizes exactly one story moment. If you find yourself writing "and then she left," that's a new beat.

**Bad beat**: "She entered the tavern, ordered a drink, and noticed Marcus in the corner."

**Good beat**: "She pushed through the tavern door, the noise swallowing her. One drink. That's all she needed. Her gaze swept the room—smoke, strangers, a musician tuning his fiddle—and landed on the corner booth where Marcus sat alone, hunched over his ale."

### No Time Jumps Within a Beat

A beat exists in continuous real-time. If the beat description says "Aria spots Marcus," the beat covers the moment of spotting, not the moment of walking over afterward.

**Red flag**: Phrases like "later," "afterward," "an hour passed," "the next day" within a single beat.

### Sensory Grounding

Anchor the moment in concrete sensory detail—not abstract summary.

**Instead of**: "She was nervous about seeing Marcus."

**Write**: "Her fingers found the ring in her pocket, turning it twice before she made herself put it down. The tavern noise pressed against her ears, too loud, too close."

### POV Interiority Calibrated to the Moment

Stay in the POV character's perspective. The reader experiences the moment through their感受, thoughts, and physical reactions.

- **Action beats**: Brief interiority—focus on the physical, the immediate
- **Reaction beats**: Deeper interiority—process what just happened
- **Dialogue beats**: Watch for subtext—what the character isn't saying
- **Internal beats**: Full interiority—this is the character's experience of their own thoughts

### Flow Into the Next Beat

End each beat with a hook or transition that leads naturally to the following beat's description.

**Pattern**: The last sentence of a beat should echo or set up the first element of the next beat's description.

```
Beat description: "Aria spots Marcus in the corner, approaches carefully."
Beat prose ends: "...she picked up her drink and moved toward the corner booth."

Next beat description: "Marcus recognizes Orin and tensions spike."
Beat prose begins: "She was three steps from the table when the door swung open again."
```

## Common Beat Types

### Action Beat
Dramatizes physical action or movement. Keep interiority brief, focus on the physical and immediate.

### Reaction Beat
Shows a character processing something that happened. Deeper interiority, moment of thinking/feeling before responding.

### Dialogue Beat
Primarily conversation between characters. Use dialogue tags sparingly (he said, she asked). Focus on subtext—what's not being said.

### Internal Beat
Character alone with their thoughts. Deep interiority. Often used at scene turning points or emotional peaks.

### Description Beat
Sets up a new location or character introduction. Grounded in sensory detail, filtered through POV perception.

## Applying the Style Guide

The style guide (`Style/master.md`) contains prose writing directives. When writing beats:

1. **Read the style guide before writing each beat**
2. **Apply directives actively**:
   - If the guide says "Use short sentences during action," vary your sentence length during physical moments
   - If it says "Avoid adverbs," proofread for -ly words and revise
   - If it says "Ground dialogue in gesture," add physical beats between lines

3. **Adapt tone to scene context** while maintaining the style guide's voice
4. **The style guide is directive, not descriptive**—it tells you what to do, not just what the project does

## The 500-Word Previous Scene Tail

When loading context, you receive the last ~500 words of the previous scene's prose (if it exists). This is for:

**Continuity of voice**: Maintaining consistent sentence rhythms, word choices, and narrative tone from where the previous scene ended.

**Not for**: Plot recap. Don't summarize what happened in the previous scene—assume the reader already knows. Pick up the new scene's prose as a new moment, not an explanation of the last one.

**How to use it**: Glance at the voice and rhythm, then write your beat in a matching tone. If the previous scene ended with short, punchy sentences, your beat might continue that energy or deliberately shift for contrast—but you should be conscious of the choice.

## Red Flags

### Beats That Summarize Instead of Dramatize

**Warning signs**:
- The prose reads like plot summary rather than scene
- "And then" constructions
- Telling emotions instead of showing them
- Scene-y feeling instead of felt experience

**Fix**: Ask "What would the POV character see, hear, feel in this moment?" and write that.

### Beats That Jump Time

**Warning signs**:
- "Later," "afterward," "an hour passed," "the next day"
- Ellipses (...) used to skip time
- Large swaths of summarized time between beats

**Fix**: Each beat covers continuous real-time. If time jumps, split into separate beats.

### Beats That Ignore POV Interiority

**Warning signs**:
- Multiple characters' thoughts shown in one beat
- Camera-eye narration ("Marcus felt angry. Aria grew suspicious.")
- Head-hopping

**Fix**: Stay in one character's perspective for the entire beat. If another character's interiority is needed, save it for when they're the POV character.

### Beats That Don't Flow

**Warning signs**:
- The beat ends on one thing, the next beat's description starts with something unrelated
- No hook or transition at beat boundaries
- The narrative feels choppy at the seams

**Fix**: Read the next beat's description before finishing the current one. The last sentence of your beat should lead naturally into the next beat's subject.

---

## Summary Checklist

Before marking a beat complete, verify:
- [ ] One moment, continuous real-time
- [ ] Sensory grounding (concrete details, not abstract)
- [ ] POV interiority (staying in one character's perspective)
- [ ] Style guide applied
- [ ] Flows into the next beat
- [ ] No beat headers in prose (beats are invisible in final text)
