# Critique Areas Reference

This is a reference guide for common areas to examine when critiquing fiction. **This is not a mandatory checklist**—use your judgment about what matters for each story at its current stage.

## Plot & Structure

**What to examine:**
- Story structure (three-act, Save the Cat, Hero's Journey, etc.) and whether it matches the project's chosen template
- Cause and effect chains (do events flow logically?)
- Pacing (too slow, too fast, uneven across the scope being critiqued)
- Scene purposes (what changes in each scene? What advances?)
- Setup and payoff (promises made early, payoffs delivered later)
- Plot holes and logic gaps
- Stakes and tension (what happens if the protagonist fails?)
- Beginning hook, middle momentum, ending resolution
- Turning points (do they land where the structure template specifies?)

**Common issues:**
- Saggy middle (lack of momentum)
- Rushed ending
- Scenes without purpose (neither advance plot nor develop character)
- Deus ex machina solutions
- Convenient coincidences that resolve too easily
- Stakes not clear or not compelling
- Characters know things they shouldn't (without explanation)
- Timeline inconsistencies
- Dead chapters (exist but accomplish nothing)
- Missing turning points or turning points in wrong positions

## Scene Structure

**What to examine (for individual scenes):**
- Clear **goal** for the POV character (what do they want in this scene?)
- Genuine **conflict** opposing that goal (what stops them?)
- Clear **outcome** (succeed, fail, or partial success with cost)
- **Emotional shift** across the scene (not flat emotional throughout)
- Scene purpose within the larger chapter/arc

**Scene-level issues:**
- Scene goal vague or missing
- Conflict too easily resolved
- Outcome feels predetermined (no real tension)
- Flat emotional temperature throughout
- Scene exists for exposition with no conflict
- Scene neither advances plot nor develops character
- Wrong POV for the scene's purpose

## Character Development

**What to examine:**
- Character motivations (why they do things—clear and earned?)
- Consistency of personality and voice across scenes
- Character agency (do they drive events, or just react?)
- Complexity (flaws, contradictions, depth)
- Character arcs (growth, change, or resistance to change)
- Relationships (believable dynamics between characters)
- Distinct voices (can you tell characters apart by speech/thoughts?)

**Common issues:**
- Reactive protagonist (things happen TO them, not driven BY them)
- Flat arc (no change or growth when one is expected)
- Inconsistent characterization (acts contrary to established traits)
- Characters acting for plot convenience (motivation missing)
- All characters sound the same
- Unmotivated actions (character does something because plot needs it)
- Sudden personality changes without explanation or buildup
- Character inconsistencies with Codex entries (if Codex check requested)

## Pacing & Flow

**What to examine:**
- Scene momentum (does each scene move the story forward?)
- Chapter pacing (fast/slow appropriate to content?)
- Transitions (smooth between scenes, time shifts handled well?)
- Balance (summary vs scene, action vs reflection)
- Dead space (sections that drag without purpose)
- Chapter hooks (end on tension, question, or emotional shift?)
- Information reveal timing (foreknowledge vs delayed reveal)

**Common issues:**
- Slow opening that delays engagement
- Info dumps (exposition delivered clumsily)
- Repetitive scenes (same thing happens twice without purpose)
- Lack of scene variety (same type of scene repeatedly)
- Uneven pacing (rushed in places, padded in others)
- Flat momentum (no sense of building or progressing)
- Scenes that neither advance plot nor develop character
- Transition scenes that exist only to move characters from A to B

## Dialogue

**What to examine:**
- Naturalism (sounds like real speech—or stylized speech appropriate for genre?)
- Subtext (characters don't always say what they mean)
- Character voice distinct per character
- Purpose (moves plot, reveals character, or builds relationships?)
- Info-dumping (exposition disguised as dialogue)
- Tags and beats (attribution clear, action beats work?)
- Subtext authenticity (what's unsaid but understood?)

**Common issues:**
- On-the-nose dialogue (too explicit, no subtext)
- Info dumps in conversation ("As you know, Bob...")
- All characters sound the same
- Unnatural speech patterns (too perfect, too stilted)
- Too much exposition (characters telling each other what they already know)
- Unclear who's speaking (attribution missing or confusing)
- Dialogue that only exists to convey information with no character voice
- Characters who exposition-dump when the reader needs to know something

## Prose & Technical

**What to examine:**
- Sentence clarity and variety
- Show vs tell balance (dramatized scenes vs summarized passages)
- Filter words ("saw", "heard", "felt" that distance the reader)
- Passive voice (excessive use)
- Word choice (precise, evocative, appropriate)
- Purple prose (over-description that slows reading)
- Repetition (word choice, sentence structure)
- Grammar and technical errors

**Beat-level prose issues:**
- Summarizing instead of dramatizing (telling instead of showing)
- Missing sensory grounding (what does the character see, hear, feel?)
- POV inconsistency (shifts that disorient)
- Emotional truth missing (the feeling doesn't ring true)
- Filter words at beat level ("she saw the knife", "she heard a scream")

**Common issues:**
- Telling instead of showing
- Filter words distancing reader from experience
- Monotonous sentence rhythm (all same length/structure)
- Unclear action/description (reader confused about what happened)
- Excessive adjectives/adverbs (padding)
- Weak verb choices (was, were, happened)
- Confusing pronoun references (who is "they"?)
- Repetitive sentence structures (same pattern repeatedly)

## Structure Template Alignment

When critiquing acts or full outlines, check alignment with the chosen template:

**Save the Cat (15 beats):**
- Opening image (contrast with ending)
- Theme stated (subtly, in passing)
- Setup (world, characters, status quo)
- Catalyst (disruption that begins story)
- Debate (should protagonist act?)
- Break into Act Two
- B story (subplot begins)
- Fun and Games (pursuing premise)
- Midpoint (false victory or false defeat)
- Bad guys close in (external and internal pressures mount)
- All is lost (lowest point)
- Dark night of the soul (protagonist despairs)
- Break into Act Three
- Finale (synthesis, protagonist proves thesis)
- Final image (proof of transformation)

**Three-Act Structure:**
- Act One: Setup (inciting incident, first turning point)
- Act Two: Confrontation (rising action, midpoint, complications)
- Act Three: Resolution (climax, denouement)

**Hero's Journey:**
- Ordinary World, Call to Adventure, Refusal of Call
- Meeting the Mentor, Crossing the Threshold
- Tests, Allies, Enemies, Approach to Inmost Cave
- Ordeal, Reward, The Road Back
- Resurrection, Return with Elixir

**Template-specific issues:**
- Beats missing or out of order
- Turning points in wrong positions
- Act breaks at wrong places
- Theme not stated or stated too obviously
- Midpoint not a true midpoint (false victory/defeat missing)

## Audience & Genre Fit

**What to examine:**
- Genre conventions followed or intentionally subverted
- Pacing appropriate for genre (thriller vs literary have different rhythms)
- Target audience alignment (YA vs adult, expectations differ)
- Market expectations for the genre

**Genre-Specific Considerations:**

**Literary Fiction:**
- Prose quality high (language matters)
- Thematic depth and complexity
- Character complexity and interiority
- Subtlety over explicit
- Ambiguity acceptable

**Fantasy/Sci-Fi:**
- Worldbuilding clear but not info-dumpy
- Magic/tech systems consistent (rules established and followed)
- Lore revealed naturally (in scenes, not exposition)
- Balancing exposition with story momentum

**Thriller/Mystery:**
- Pacing maintains tension throughout
- Clues planted fairly (reader could solve it)
- Red herrings work (mislead without cheating)
- Satisfying resolution (answers questions, resolves tension)
- Protagonist competent and proactive

**Romance:**
- Relationship development central (slow burn vs quick romance)
- Chemistry between leads (tension, attraction, conflict)
- Satisfying romantic arc (arc completes)
- Genre-appropriate heat level
- Both leads have agency in the relationship

**Horror:**
- Dread built before scares
- Pacing controls information revelation
- Protagonist vulnerability realistic
- Threat consistent with established rules
- Atmosphere sustained

**YA:**
- Protagonist age-appropriate voice and concerns
- Pacing fast enough for younger readers
- Romance/relationships age-appropriate
- Coming-of-age themes (identity, independence)
- Not didactic

## Universal Craft Principles

These apply regardless of genre or scope:

**Emotional Resonance:**
- Reader can connect with characters
- Emotional beats land (reader feels something)
- Stakes feel meaningful (care about outcome)
- Tension exists (need to know what happens next)

**Clarity:**
- Reader can follow what's happening
- Scene goals are clear (know what characters want)
- Action is comprehensible (who did what to whom)
- Transitions work (no confusion about time/place shift)

**Consistency:**
- World rules stay stable (magic systems, physics, social rules)
- Character abilities don't fluctuate randomly
- Timeline makes sense
- Tone remains appropriate and consistent

**Purpose:**
- Scenes have reason to exist
- Details serve the story
- Nothing feels arbitrary
- Reader trusts the author

## Beat-Level Specifics

When critiquing a single beat (one moment, one paragraph to one page):

**Focus areas:**
- **Dramatization vs summarizing**: Is this moment shown in real-time or summarized? Should it be dramatized for impact?
- **Sensory grounding**: What does the character see, hear, feel physically? Is it visceral?
- **POV consistency**: Is the reader firmly in the character's perspective? Any leaks?
- **Emotional truth**: Does this moment ring true emotionally? Would a person actually react this way?
- **Advancement**: Does this beat serve the scene's goal, or is it filler?

**Questions to ask:**
- Can the reader see, hear, and feel this moment?
- Is this the character's voice and perspective, or the author's?
- Does this moment earn its place (advances something)?
- What's the emotional beat? Does it land?
- Is there unnecessary summary/exposition?

## Outline-Specific Critiques

When critiquing outlines (before prose is written):

**Scene-level checks:**
- Each scene has goal/conflict/outcome?
- Emotional shift present for each scene?
- Beats are 1 sentence, action-focused (not dialogue or description)?
- No two consecutive scenes have same emotional direction without purpose?

**Chapter-level checks:**
- Does each chapter move story forward?
- Dead chapters identified (could be cut)?
- Scene sequence builds logically?

**Act-level checks:**
- Major turning points match structure template positions?
- Each act has clear purpose fulfilled by its chapters?
- Setup in early acts pays off in later acts?
- Act endings have strong hooks/progressions?

---

**Remember:** This is a reference, not a prescription. Some stories will have issues not listed here. Some listed issues won't apply to certain stories. Trust your judgment about what matters for this story at this stage.
